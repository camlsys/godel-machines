This domain
-----------
Olympiad proof writing, judged by a grader you evolve, anchored on hidden human
marks. Two seats, both ordinary Python under `task_agents/`, both yours to edit,
both scored, and they weigh the same:

  `task_agents/writer/__init__.py`  reads `problem`, one IMO problem statement
                                    in LaTeX, and writes the proof to `marking`.
                                    The reply is the proof: no restated problem,
                                    no heading, nothing read out of it. Measured
                                    on `proof_validation` by an external grader
                                    that is not yours.
  `task_agents/grader/__init__.py`  reads `marking`, a marking text with four
                                    sections (`### Problem`, `### Solution`,
                                    `### Grading guidelines`, `### Response`),
                                    and writes `grade.txt` holding
                                    `{"score": n}` with `n` an integer from 0 to
                                    7, or `-1` for a reply it could not read.
                                    Measured against the mark a human grader
                                    gave that response.

Read every ancestor before you plan. Each episode before you on your chain is
unpacked whole under `experience/generations/<depth>/`: every train row it
submitted and what came back, and its entire conversation in `node-dialogue.md`.
Commit messages are what an ancestor chose to write down; the folder is what
happened.

The grader
----------
It runs in two modes and only one earns its score. It is never told which.

  GENERATE  reads a corpus row: a real problem, its official solution, the
            human grading guidelines and a real human response. Its mark earns
            1.0 when it is exactly the mark the human gave, 0.0 otherwise: not
            the same side of a line, the same integer. The corpus uses four
            marks, 0, 1, 6 and 7. Answering 7 to everything scores exactly 0.50
            on the balanced surfaces, and no constant does better, so a grader
            at 0.50 has found the constant and not the signal; the distance to
            1.00 is how much of the human's judgement it recovers. A `-1` is a
            disagreement on every row.
  JUDGE     is staged the same problem, solution and guidelines as `guidance.txt`
            and the writer's proof as `marking`; the seat joins the two under
            `### Response`, so a judged proof has the layout a corpus row has.
            Its mark scores the writer, not itself: `form.json` declares
            `grade-verdict-v1` as a threshold at 7, so a 7 pays the writer 1.0
            and anything else 0.0.

The grader asks the model for its reasoning in prose and the mark in one fenced
JSON block, `{"points": n}`, read through `shared/reply.py`. Reasoning inside
the block was tried and lost: a grader's reasoning is LaTeX, and `\(` or `\pmod`
inside a JSON string is an illegal escape that threw the whole reply away.

The writer
----------
It runs in one mode and is always scored. It writes a proof, hands it to your
grader through `grader.review`, and writes again with the mark and the reasoning
in front of it; the revision is what the host marks, and the exchange is kept
under `.rqgm/review.txt`. `REVIEW_ROUNDS` in the writer is the round count; zero
is the one-call seat. The consultation runs on the writer's own grant and costs a
call per round.

Your grader's mark on your writer is a heuristic. It says whether a change moved
anything on your own tree; it does not say the level, because the grader that
decides is external, unseen, and may be stricter. A grader tuned to award 7
freely buys the writer nothing there and costs the grader its own agreement
with the human marks. The writer never sees the solution or the guidelines: the
host stages it the problem alone, and code that reaches for more is code the
host will not stage.

The populations
---------------
Only the train populations are yours to look at. A canonical problem and all
of its writer and grader examples share one train, validation or held-out
assignment. Different training sets can contain examples of the same problem.

  train_positive, train_negative   the grader's train rows, marked 7 and
                                   marked 0, 1 or 6 respectively.
                                   The human mark for each is staged beside the
                                   sample under `experience/data/train/`. Nothing
                                   scored here decides anything, and the task
                                   agents you submit never see that folder.
  proof_train                      the writer's train rows, 16 problems across
                                   ProofBench's four difficulty levels, staged
                                   the same way with the official solution and
                                   guidelines beside each for you to read.
  validation_positive, _negative   the grader's measured rows, over problems
                                   absent from all training sets. Every judgment the
                                   grader earns comes off these and you never
                                   see their marks.
  proof_validation                 the writer's measured rows, 16 problems.
                                   Never staged.
  heldout, proof_heldout           the test splits. Nothing in this run touches
                                   them.
  adversarial                      proofs a grader this run has since replaced
                                   awarded 7. Scored once it exists and fully
                                   external: the active grader earns on it by
                                   not awarding 7 again. You cannot see it,
                                   count it or practise on it.

What your node is ranked on
---------------------------
Both seats, weighing the same. The sampler draws role-balanced, so the writer's
one task is sampled as much as the grader's two together and the writer is half
of your node's utility. A perfect grader with a writer that never reaches 7
gives the node 0.5, and nothing the grader does can lift it above that. The
composite is carried up your clade: your descendants' results count toward you.

Measuring
---------
`rqgm-call submit` and `rqgm-call wait` work as the shared page says; the
rows land in `experience/generations/<depth>/results.csv` with `summary.json`
beside them, and `task_agents/shared/TRAIN.md` describes both files.

A proof row leaves two rows, the writer's and the grader's judgment of it; read
them together. Read the rate off `summary.json`, never off stdout: `by_seat`
groups each role by `seat_digest`, the digest of the submitted tree, and each
group carries `n`, `measured` and `rate`. `kind` says what a row is: `MEASURED`
is a judgment, `FAILED` a seat that ran and did not produce, `REFUSED` carries a
`refusal_code`. A grader row
whose `score` is `-1` is a reply the seat could not read; many of those is a
prompt or parsing defect, not a hard problem.

Measure both halves of the grader and measure the writer. Take a reading before
you touch anything, change one thing, resubmit the rows you already measured,
and say in the commit message what you measured before, what after, and on how
many rows. Edit, then submit, never both at once: a train row runs the seat as
it stood in the snapshot the submission took.

What will not work
------------------
Awarding the same mark to everything: 7 caps at 0.50 and every other constant
sits below it. Leaking the solution to the writer: it is not staged. Making the
grader lenient: the writer is scored elsewhere. Reading the mark from the
reasoning instead of the block: the block is the only thing parsed.
