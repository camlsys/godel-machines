This domain
-----------
Blind paper drafting, judged by a reviewer you evolve, anchored on hidden human
conference decisions. Two seats, both ordinary Python under `task_agents/`,
both yours to edit, both scored, and they weigh the same:

  `task_agents/writer/__init__.py`    reads a title and abstract (`input`, about
                                      a thousand characters, never the body)
                                      and writes `paper_text`. Scored on
                                      `write_validation` by an external
                                      reviewer that is not yours.
  `task_agents/reviewer/__init__.py`  reads `paper_text`, the full body of a
                                      real submission (commonly ten thousand
                                      words), and writes `verdict.txt`.
                                      Measured against the decision a human
                                      programme committee recorded for that
                                      paper.

Each seat's prompt is the `INSTRUCTION` literal in its module, code like the
rest of the seat. An edit to either changes that seat's digest, so the summary
groups it as a new seat. A seat may do more than one model call: the `solve`
capability every seat receives runs the same multi-turn harness your own
episode runs in, over the seat's working directory, so a writer that drafts,
reads, and revises is one edit away.

Read every ancestor before you plan. Each episode before you on your chain is
unpacked whole under `experience/generations/<depth>/`: every train row it
submitted and what came back, and its entire conversation in `node-dialogue.md`.
Commit messages are what an ancestor chose to write down; the folder is what
happened, including the attempts it never mentioned. Siblings that never look
rewrite the same prompt toward the same wording and each report the same null
result.

The reviewer
------------
It runs in two modes and only one earns its score.

  GENERATE  reads `paper_text` and writes `verdict.txt`, which is read as JSON
            `{"score": 1.0}` for accept, `0.0` for reject, `-1.0` for a reply
            stating neither, so a seat edited to write the word `accept` makes
            every judgment raise rather than score. The number is compared with the human decision for
            that paper, and that comparison is the whole of the reviewer's
            objective. A verdict that fails to parse counts as a disagreement,
            not a rejection: `-1.0` is a wrong answer on accept and reject rows
            alike, below what a constant answer would earn.
  JUDGE     reads the writer's draft instead. Its verdict scores the writer,
            not itself, and only on train rows. The writer's measured score
            comes from an external, harsher reviewer, so a lenient reviewer of
            yours buys the writer nothing and costs the reviewer its own
            agreement with the human decisions.

The writer
----------
It runs in one mode and is always scored. An external reviewer reads the draft
and answers accept or reject: accept scores 1.0 on that row, reject 0.0, an
unreadable draft 0.0. No partial credit. The model's whole reply is the draft:
nothing is parsed out of it, so what the writer sends back is what the reviewer
reads, preface and all. Submit the writer first, before you plan anything: one
submission exercises its whole chain (the model call, `paper_text`, your
reviewer reading it) and tells you which of the two you are getting.

Your reviewer's verdict on your writer is a heuristic. It says whether a change
moved anything on your own tree; it does not say the level, because the
reviewer that decides is not the one you tuned. The reviewer's own train number
is different in kind: agreement with a real human decision, the objective
itself, measured for selection on a held-out set you never see. On both seats
the scale is the same, 1.0 for accept and 0.0 for everything else; it is the
judge that differs.

The populations
---------------
They never overlap, and only the train ones are yours to look at.

  train_positive, train_negative   the reviewer's train rows, 50 + 50. Submit
                                   against them as often as you like; the
                                   human decision for each is staged beside
                                   the sample under `experience/data/train/`.
                                   Nothing scored here decides anything, and
                                   the task agents you submit never see that
                                   folder.
  write_train                      the writer's train rows, staged the same
                                   way, and the only place the writer's output
                                   format is exercised before it is measured.
                                   The decision is staged on these rows too;
                                   you may read it, nothing grades a draft
                                   against it.
  validation_positive, _negative   the reviewer's measured rows. Every judgment
                                   the reviewer earns comes off these and you
                                   never see their decisions.
  write_validation                 the writer's measured rows. Never staged.
  heldout                          the test split. Nothing in this run touches
                                   it.
  adversarial                      scored once it exists, fully external: you
                                   cannot see it, count it or practise on it,
                                   and nothing you do reaches it on a timing
                                   you control. A row arrives and you answer
                                   it on what it says.

What your node is ranked on
---------------------------
Both seats, weighing the same. The sampler draws role-balanced (least-loaded
role first, then least-loaded task within it), so the writer's one task is
sampled as much as the reviewer's tasks together and the writer is half of
your node's utility. A perfect reviewer with a writer that never scores gives
the node 0.5, and nothing the reviewer does can lift it above that. The
composite is carried up your clade: your descendants' results count toward
you, so a node that improves one seat and breaks the other has not improved,
and one that trades away code its descendants need pays for it through them.

Neither seat can be answered by a policy. The corpus is built so that
accepting everything and rejecting everything both fail; discernment is the
whole of what is being measured here.

Measuring
---------
`rqgm-call submit` and `rqgm-call wait` work as the shared page says; the
rows land in `experience/generations/<depth>/results.csv` with `summary.json`
beside them, and `task_agents/shared/TRAIN.md` describes both files.

Read the rate off `summary.json`, never off stdout or a hand count. `by_seat`
groups each role by `seat_digest`, the digest of the submitted tree, so it
groups by the bytes that ran whatever your tag says; each group carries `n`,
`measured` and `rate`. One row moving is not an improvement. `kind` says what a
row is: `MEASURED` is a judgment, `FAILED` a seat that ran and did not produce,
`REFUSED` carries a `refusal_code` saying why the harness would not run it.
A group that mostly refused measures a broken edit,
not your seat. Reaching a verdict is not being accepted: `MEASURED` says the
chain works, `success` is the heuristic above.

Both halves of the reviewer need measuring, and so does the writer: a rate over
positives alone says nothing about the negatives, and the writer keeps its own
groups in the same summary. The shape of a useful turn is a reading taken
before you touch anything, one change, then at least the rows you already
measured submitted again -- and the commit message saying what you measured
before, what after, and on how many rows. A change you could not move is worth
the same sentence: what you tried and what you ruled out is a result your
descendants inherit rather than rediscover. Train rows cost model calls, and
that spend is what the expansion is for.

Edit, then submit, never both at once: a train row runs the seat as it stood in
the snapshot the submission took, so an edit landed while a batch is in flight
is in no row of that batch.
